# NPL recovery strategy for Bank XYZ

**Result:** a two-phase plan. Phase 1 (Nov to Dec 2026) cuts NPL before year-end with six steps. Phase 2 (2027) splits the analyst role and adds an early-warning score so repair stops competing with growth.

> Bank XYZ is anonymised. Branch caps and interest tiers are illustrative; final limits are set per group.

## Context
- Two months left in the year: decisions and contracts must close before the books do.
- Each credit analyst handles lending, restructuring and collection at the same time.
- Lending growth targets compete with portfolio repair for the same analyst's time.

## Problem
| Issue | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Misaligned KPIs | NPL weight is 20% at head office and regions but only 10% at branches and individuals |
| Slow approvals | Long restructuring memos and committee chains risk missing the year-end deadline |
| Reactive collection | No scoring flags debtors before they slip into NPL |

**Problem statement:** how can the bank cut NPL by year-end without hurting profit or compliance, and stop it from building up again?

## Data
KPI weight by level:

| Level | Lending | NPL | Funding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Head office | 30% | 20% | 10% |
| Region | 30% | 20% | 10% |
| Branch | 20% | 10% | 20% |
| Individual | 20% | 10% | 20% |

Branch segmentation by NPL (as of 31 Oct 2026). Capped branches must resolve a set share of their NPL before lending resumes.

| Branch NPL | Lending rule |
| --- | --- |
| above 5% | Lending stopped |
| 4% to 5% | Lending capped (e.g. 40%) |
| 3% to 4% | Lending capped (e.g. 20%) |
| below 3% | Normal lending |

**Why restructuring beats leaving it in NPL:** a performing loan books 100% of interest as revenue; an NPL earns 0%. In the example, a restructured loan at 13.5% pays 3% now as revenue, and the remaining 10.5% is deferred interest (IBP) repaid in tiers. Two groups (DSR 5% and DSR 10%) step the rate up every 3 months and return to normal after 12 or 9 months. Projections run under several scenarios to year-end.

## Solution
**Phase 1 (Nov to Dec 2026): six steps**

1. **Sync KPIs.** Align the NPL weight from head office down to individuals.
2. **Segment branches.** Stop or cap lending by NPL band.
3. **Simplify approvals.** One short memo; approver tied to the IBP.
4. **Tier the IBP.** Clear steps and a deadline back to normal rate.
5. **Restructure selectively.** Voluntary, documented, multiple schemes.
6. **Align stakeholders.** Risk, audit, finance and the board agree upfront.

8-week timeline:

| Week | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Nov week 1 | KPI check, portfolio grouping, pre-talks with finance, compliance and the board |
| Nov week 2 | Profit scenarios, IBP policy, memo and consent templates |
| Nov week 3 | Branch briefing, priority memos, committees |
| Nov week 4 to Dec | Committees and contract signing in alternation |

**Phase 2 (2027): split the role and catch arrears early**
- **Growth Analyst:** healthy new lending, own KPIs. **Recovery Analyst:** restructuring, early warning, collection, own KPIs.
- **Early-warning payment-behaviour score:** pays before due date, needs a reminder or visit, one instalment in arrears. The monthly score sets the collection channel and priority.
- **30-day collection cycle:** first call day 10, second call day 20, final day 30.

## Expected impact and the KPIs to prove it
| Lever | Expected effect | KPI to track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Branch segmentation | No new bad loans from high-NPL branches | NPL ratio per branch group by 31 Dec |
| Short memo and committee | Restructurings signed before year-end | Contracts signed vs target per week |
| Tiered IBP | Interest income instead of 0% on NPL | Interest from restructured loans vs projection |
| Voluntary, documented consent | Restructuring that holds up to audit | Audit findings on restructuring files |
| Split roles and early warning (2027) | Arrears caught before they turn NPL | Roll rate to NPL; collection rate per cycle |

Projected impact of the proposed plan; KPIs are measured as the plan is executed.
