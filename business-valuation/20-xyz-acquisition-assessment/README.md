# XYZ: a five-part acquisition and funding assessment

**Result:** XYZ, a home-services company, earns a thin and unstable profit (EBITDA margin 1.9%) and is weak on liquidity and leverage. An asking price of Rp 2.0 billion for 51% is about four times fair value on current numbers, so do not buy at that price. A Rp 2.0 billion, 11-month facility is conditionally feasible, but only if collections stay near 100%.

> Anonymised case. Company and investor names are replaced (XYZ, "the investor"); amounts are restated to full Rupiah and shown in Rp million.

## The case
XYZ provides maintenance, cleaning, AC service and repair for homes, shops and buildings. An analyst receives four transaction tables for January to June 2025 (sales, purchases, payroll, transport), a year-end balance sheet, and five questions: summarise the finances, judge the condition, build a cash flow, value an offer for 51% at Rp 2.0 billion, and assess a Rp 2.0 billion funding request tied to a new two-year contract.

## How the parts connect
Part 1 builds the numbers, part 2 judges them, part 3 shows that liquidity depends on collecting receivables, part 4 values the stake on that weak base and part 5 tests whether a new contract changes the picture. The contract is the swing factor in both the valuation and the funding case, which is why the recommendations pay for it only when it is verified.

## Model notes and corrections
- **Bank interest:** the original analysis counted Rp 270 M a year; it left out 12% on the Rp 2.0 bn long-term loan. The correct figure is Rp 510 M. Debt service coverage is 0.64x, not 0.84x, and funding cash is Rp 220 M lower over 11 months. No conclusion changes; a switch cell reproduces the original figures.
- **Assumptions:** the 5x comparable EV/EBITDA multiple is an assumption, not a sourced comparable. A "15% to 25% typical EBITDA margin" claim in the original had no source and is not used.
- **Data flag:** June has 87 payroll lines but 86 unique employee IDs (possible duplicate, under 1.5% of June salaries).
